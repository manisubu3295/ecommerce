How your day works

One artist, from the trial to the last family photo

Nothing about the wedding morning is improvised. This is roughly how a muhurtham booking runs.

  1. Two weeks before

    The trial

    Ninety minutes at the studio. We test two bases, settle an eye and a lip against a photo of your first outfit, and shoot it in daylight and in flash.

  2. Once you book

    Face mapping

    Notes and product choices are saved to your file and shortlisted to each outfit, so the morning has no guesswork.

  3. Before first light

    Set up on site

    Kit laid out at the venue, lights up, kettle on. You eat something before we start — the bride is always the first chair.

  4. The first 90 minutes

    Skin, then eyes

    Prep and base while the room is quiet, eyes built to the outfit, lash last. Unhurried, and on schedule.

  5. As the light comes up

    Draping & jewellery

    The saree pinned and set, tikka and nose-ring on, and a photo for you to check before anyone else sees you.

  6. Between events

    Reception reset

    A full change of look — new eye, new lip, fresh skin — plus mother and sisters, scheduled around your slot.

The artist

The bride should recognise herself in the mirror

Maadhavi started with a borrowed kit and a cousin’s wedding in a village outside Kumbakonam. Anitha Sundaram — twelve years and Lakmé Academy training later — still works to the rule from that morning: makeup should hold for the whole day, photograph honestly, and never arrive before the bride does.

Every face is mapped at the trial. Every product is chosen for Tamil Nadu heat and eight hours in front of a camera. The studio is in Gandhi Nagar, Kumbakonam; most weeks it travels.

  • Trained at Lakmé Academy, Chennai (2013); airbrush certified for film and 4K.
  • Disposable applicators and sanitised palettes for every bride.
  • Shade range matched fair to deep — no grey, no ashy bases.
